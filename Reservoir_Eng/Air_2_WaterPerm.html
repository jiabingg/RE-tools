Important interpretation notes
  • This tool uses natural logarithms directly with the coefficients printed on the paper figures.
  • These are empirical screening correlations, not a replacement for measured water/brine permeability or SCAL data.
  • For water-sensitive or swelling-clay intervals, brine/water permeability may be much lower than air permeability.
  • The paper specifically says brine-to-air permeability averaged about 0.5 across 0.1 to 10,000 md for the composite brine dataset.
  • The clayey sandstone effective-oil model is field-specific and should not be applied blindly to other reservoirs.

How to use in a workbook or report

The general Excel form using natural log is:

=EXP(intercept + slope*LN(k_air_cell))

Example for the composite brine correlation:

=EXP(-0.2687 + 1.0165*LN(A2))

This tool reports permeability in md when the input air permeability is in md.

Built from the equations shown in Figures 1-6 of Pugh, Thomas, and Gupta (1991), using the printed coefficients directly in LN form. Coefficients checked from the paper images: Fig. 1 oil/all = 1.0258, -0.1394; Fig. 2 brine/all = 1.0165, -0.2687; Fig. 3 sandstone = 1.047, -0.834; Fig. 4 limestone = 1.0299, -0.571; Fig. 5 dolomite = 1.056, -0.503; Fig. 6 clayey effective oil = 1.2651, -2.5351. Verify applicability for your reservoir, especially for high-clay or water-sensitive formations.
