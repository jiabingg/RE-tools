# ZEI Radius Calculation — Volumetric Method ### For Water Disposal into a Depleted Oil Reservoir --- ## The Concept The volumetric method estimates the ZEI radius by treating the cumulative injected fluid as a cylinder spreading radially through the *available* pore space of the injection zone. In a depleted oil reservoir, residual oil saturation (Sor) still occupies a portion of the pore volume, so only the water-fillable fraction of the pore space is available to receive injected fluid. --- ## The Formula $$r = \sqrt{ \frac{V_{inj}}{\pi \cdot h \cdot \phi \cdot (1 - S_{or})} }$$ Where: | Symbol | Description | Units | |--------|-------------|-------| | **r** | ZEI radius | ft | | **V_inj** | Cumulative volume injected over project life | ft³ | | **h** | Net injection zone thickness | ft | | **ϕ** | Porosity (decimal) | dimensionless | | **S_or** | Residual oil saturation (decimal) | dimensionless | | **π** | 3.14159... | — | The term **ϕ × (1 − S_or)** represents the effective pore volume available to the injected water. This replaces the bare porosity (ϕ) used when the formation contains no residual hydrocarbons. --- ## Step-by-Step Procedure **Step 1 — Cumulative injection volume.** Sum total barrels over the permit life and convert to cubic feet: V_inj (ft³) = V_inj (bbl) × 5.615 **Step 2 — Net injection zone thickness (h).** From well logs or completion records — net porous/permeable interval. **Step 3 — Porosity (ϕ).** From core or log analysis. Express as decimal (e.g., 0.30). **Step 4 — Residual oil saturation (S_or).** From core flood data, log analysis, or field waterflood performance. Typical values for California heavy oil reservoirs range from 0.15 to 0.35. For light oil reservoirs, 0.10 to 0.25 is common. **Step 5 — Calculate.** r = √( V_inj / (π × h × ϕ × (1 − S_or)) ) **Step 6 — Convert.** r (miles) = r (ft) / 5,280 r (acres) = π × r² / 43,560 --- ## Example Calculation | Parameter | Value | |-----------|-------| | Injection rate | 5,000 bbl/day | | Project life | 20 years | | Net thickness (h) | 100 ft | | Porosity (ϕ) | 0.30 | | Residual oil saturation (S_or) | 0.25 | **Cumulative volume:** V = 5,000 × 365 × 20 = 36,500,000 bbl V = 36,500,000 × 5.615 = 204,947,500 ft³ **Effective pore fraction:** ϕ × (1 − S_or) = 0.30 × (1 − 0.25) = 0.30 × 0.75 = 0.225 **Radius:** r = √( 204,947,500 / (π × 100 × 0.225) ) r = √( 204,947,500 / 70.69 ) r = √( 2,899,636 ) r ≈ 1,703 ft ≈ 0.32 miles **Compare:** If S_or were ignored (ϕ alone = 0.30), the radius would be 1,475 ft. Accounting for residual oil *increases* the calculated radius because less pore space is available, so the fluid must spread further to accommodate the same volume. --- ## Sensitivity to Residual Oil Saturation Using the same example inputs but varying S_or: | S_or | Effective Pore Fraction | ZEI Radius (ft) | ZEI Radius (mi) | |------|------------------------|-----------------|-----------------| | 0.00 | 0.300 | 1,475 | 0.28 | | 0.10 | 0.270 | 1,555 | 0.29 | | 0.20 | 0.240 | 1,650 | 0.31 | | 0.25 | 0.225 | 1,703 | 0.32 | | 0.30 | 0.210 | 1,762 | 0.33 | | 0.40 | 0.180 | 1,905 | 0.36 | Higher residual oil means less room for water, which pushes the fluid front further out. --- ## Notes - If there is also residual gas saturation (S_gr), the formula becomes: **ϕ × (1 − S_or − S_gr)** - If initial water saturation (S_wi) already occupies some pore space and you are only displacing the movable fraction, some operators use **ϕ × (1 − S_or − S_wi)** for the fluid front of *new* injected water only. However, for a conservative ZEI calculation, using **(1 − S_or)** is standard because the injected water mixes with and displaces the existing connate water, and the pressure front encompasses both. - This method is conservative — it assumes radial symmetry, no vertical leakoff, no dispersion, and piston-like displacement. --- *Reference: Standard material-balance volumetric approach used in California UIC practice, in conjunction with the analytical pressure methods of Warner & Lehr (1977), EPA-600/2-77-240.*
